Understand your YouTube content performance

Beginning November 2026, YouTube public view counts on video watch pages incorporate co-views by estimating the number of individual viewers watching together on TV screens. Views are counted the moment a video starts to play across all formats, including Shorts, long-form videos (VODs), and live streams.

This update won’t impact your YouTube Partner Program (YPP) earnings or eligibility. YPP earnings will still be based on “engaged Shorts views” and “engaged watch hours”. YPP eligibility will still be based on “qualified Shorts views” and “qualified watch hours.”

The Content tab in YouTube Insights gives you an overview of how your audience finds your content, what your audience is watching, and how they interact with your content. The Content tab is only available at the channel level. Learn more about how engagement metrics are counted.

All
New viewers

This reports viewers who watched something on your channel for the first time in the selected time period.

Casual viewers

Casual viewers are viewers who have watched your channel occasionally for a while. Casual viewers have watched your channel at least once per month for one to 5 months in the past year.

Regular viewers

Regular viewers are viewers who have been watching consistently for a long time. Regular viewers have watched your channel at least once per month for more than 6 months in the past year.

Unique reach

Estimated total number of individual people who saw your video or channel. This accounts for shared viewing on devices like YouTube TV and the main YouTube app on TV.

Views (Co-viewed)

Estimated total view count including multiple people watching together.

Note: This metric is available in YouTube Insights Advanced Mode until May 2027.
Co-view rate

A dedicated metric that helps you understand how co-viewing impacts your overall view count. You can divide your total views by this rate to calculate your baseline views without co-viewers.

Subscribers

This report shows the number of subscribers that you gained from each content type: videos, Shorts, live streams, posts, and others. “Others” include subscriptions from YouTube search and your channel page. This info helps you understand what content performs best at turning viewers into subscribers.

Views

This report shows you the number of legitimate views on your content for Shorts, videos, and live streams.

Views are counted the moment a video starts to play across all formats, and incorporate co-views by estimating the number of individual viewers watching together on TV screens.
Impressions and how they led to watch time

This report shows you how many times a thumbnail was shown to viewers on YouTube (impressions), how frequently those thumbnails resulted in a view (click-through rate), and how those views ultimately led to watch time. Learn more tips for using impressions and CTR data.

Published content

This report shows the number of videos, Shorts, live streams, and posts you’ve published on YouTube.

Viewers across formats

This report shows the breakdown and overlap of viewers consuming your content by format (videos, Shorts, and live).

How viewers found your content

This report shows you how your viewers found your content within browse features, Shorts feed, suggested videos, YouTube search, channel pages, and others.

Top remixed

This report shows a visual overview of your remix views, total remixes, and top remixed content.

Metrics to know
Thumbnail impressions	

How many times your thumbnails were shown to viewers on YouTube through registered thumbnail impressions.




Thumbnail impressions click-through-rate

	

How often viewers watched a video after seeing a thumbnail.


Engaged views	

How many times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops. This does not include co-views.


Stayed to watch	The percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short.


Unique viewers

	

Estimated number of viewers that watched your content within the selected date range, based on engaged views. This does not include co-viewers.


Unique reach	Estimated total number of individual people who saw your video or channel. This accounts for shared viewing on devices like YouTube TV and the main YouTube app on TV.
Views (Co-viewed)	

Estimated total number of views, including multiple people watching together. This accounts for shared viewing on devices like YouTube TV and the main YouTube app on TV.

Note: This metric will be available until May 2027.


Co-View Rate	A dedicated metric that helps you understand how co-viewing impacts your overall view count. You can divide your total views by this rate to calculate your baseline views without co-views.


Average view duration

	

Average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch.

This is calculated from engaged views and their corresponding watch time.


Average percentage viewed

	

Average percentage of a video watched among those who stayed to watch.

This is calculated from engaged views and their corresponding watch time.




Watch time (hours)

	

The amount of time viewers have watched your video. This includes watch time from co-views.


Post likes	The amount of likes your post received.
Post like rate	The percentage of viewers that liked your post.
Views from playlist	Video views from viewers watching the playlist itself. This is the same metric available externally on the public playlist page.
Total views	Total views of all the videos in the playlist, regardless of whether they’re from the playlist or elsewhere. This is only computed for videos that you owned in the playlist.
Follow-on Views	Organic views you get when someone watches your videos after seeing your promoted content.
